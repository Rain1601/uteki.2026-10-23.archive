1. Establish reporting context before extracting.
Identify the legal issuer, consolidated versus parent-only scope, form and report edition, accounting framework, presentation currency and scale, fiscal labels and actual reporting dates. 2. Extract the requested core financial package.

3. Read tables as financial statements.
Read row labels, column headers, notes and unit captions together. Preserve the source numeral and sign in value; copy a range into value and upper. Parentheses may express a negative amount. A dash or blank is not automatically zero. unit must retain the actual printed scale, such as USD_millions, TWD_thousands or shares_millions. Do not convert currencies. A convenience US-dollar translation is distinct from the reporting-currency amount; prioritize the requested reporting currency and label any additional translation explicitly. Share counts, par value, weighted-average shares and balance-sheet capital are distinct measures. Do not mix consolidated and standalone tables or annual and quarterly/YTD columns.

Use actual block_id values and exact contiguous source-language quotes. Provide separate evidence entries for the row/value, period header and unit/scope caption when they occur in different places. 5. Map conservatively and preserve raw findings.
Use catalog IDs only when the source wording and scope support them. Preserve the source metric/entity/period wording; choose null for unmapped IDs and retain the draft with unresolved_fields rather than forcing a match. Actual financial observations, guidance and attributed narrative statements are different kinds. Do not turn a management explanation into established causation. Do not infer a separate business entity from a name. Do not compute missing margins, growth, fiscal boundaries, currency translations or expected results.

Check each requested metric and each applicable presented period. Detect duplicate/repeated disclosures without adding them; retain conflicts and restated versions with provenance.
